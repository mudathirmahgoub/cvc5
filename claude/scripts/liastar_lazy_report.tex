\title{Engineering a Lazy Decision Procedure for \textsc{LIA}$^{*}$ in \cvc:\\
Strategies, Algorithms, and an Experimental Account}
\author{Claude (Anthropic)}
\date{June 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We describe a sequence of engineering and algorithmic improvements to the
lazy decision procedure for the \emph{additive closure} (star) operator over
linear integer arithmetic implemented in \cvc, following the
approximation-based approach of Levatich et al.\ (VMCAI~2020). Starting from
an implementation that timed out on the hardest instances of a
BAPA/MAPA-derived benchmark family, we develop and evaluate seven
strategies: subsolver refinement under push/pop, main-solver cell
enumeration, propositional and semantic cell generalization, constant-size
reduction lemmas via partial sums, decomposition-guided cell discovery with
a decoupled endgame and decision-strategy witness feedback for satisfiable
instances, and counterexample-guided synthesis of over-approximating
homogeneous cuts for unsatisfiable instances. Each step was driven by a
measured failure of the previous one; we report the negative results
alongside the positive ones. The final configuration solves all 480
benchmarks within a 100-second timeout (median well under one second),
including instances on which every reference solver in our comparison set
timed out, with no answer discrepancies.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem and Background}

\subsection{The \starc{} operator}

A literal
\[
\ell \;=\; \mathit{int.star\text{-}contains}\big(\lambda x_1 \ldots x_n.\; p(\mathbf{x}),\; v_1,\ldots,v_n\big)
\]
asserts that the vector $\vvec = (v_1,\ldots,v_n)$ of integer terms belongs
to the \emph{star} (additive closure) of the set
\[
\Sset \;=\; \{\, \mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{Z}^n \mid p(\mathbf{x}) \wedge \mathbf{x} \ge 0 \,\},
\qquad
\Sstar \;=\; \Big\{\, \textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{k} \mathbf{s}_j \;\Big|\; k \ge 0,\; \mathbf{s}_j \in \Sset \,\Big\},
\]
where the empty sum yields the zero vector. The sets $\Sstar$ are exactly
the non-negative semilinear sets; the operator arises when encoding BAPA
(Boolean algebra with Presburger arithmetic) and multiset (MAPA)
constraints, where each summand abstracts one Venn-region cardinality
pattern.

\subsection{The reduction of Levatich et al.}

The decision procedure reduces $\ell$ to plain linear integer arithmetic:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Normalize $p$ into DNF, $p \Leftrightarrow D_1 \vee \cdots \vee D_m$;
  each disjunct (a \emph{cell}) is a convex polyhedron, so
  $\Sset = S_1 \cup \cdots \cup S_m$.
\item The closure distributes over union as a Minkowski sum:
  $\Sstar = S_1^{*} + \cdots + S_m^{*}$, since the summands of any sum can be
  grouped by the cell they came from.
\item For each cell, \normaliz computes \emph{module generators} $g$ and a
  \emph{Hilbert basis} $\{h_k\}$ of its recession cone, so its integer
  points are $\{\, g + \sum_k l_k h_k \mid l_k \ge 0 \,\}$. The semigroup
  $S_j^{*}$ is captured by a multiplier $\mu_g \ge 0$ per generator (how
  many copies are taken) and $l_k \ge 0$ per ray, coupled by
  $\mu_g = 0 \Rightarrow l_k = 0$.
\item Summing the contributions over all cells gives the \emph{star
  constraints}: a linear system over fresh multipliers whose satisfiability
  is equivalent to $\vvec \in \Sstar$. The procedure emits the reduction
  lemma $\ell \Leftrightarrow \mathit{star}$.
\end{enumerate}

The \emph{eager} strategy builds the whole DNF up front; since the DNF
explodes (the benchmark predicates contain up to 23 if-then-else terms), the
\emph{lazy} strategy instead discovers one cell per refinement round, from a
model of a region not yet covered, and asserts the equivalence only
\emph{tentatively}: while cells are missing, $\mathit{star}_k$
under-approximates $\Sstar$, so the lemma $g_k \Rightarrow (\ell
\Leftrightarrow \mathit{star}_k)$ is guarded by a fresh Boolean decision
variable $g_k$ biased to true, and the previous guard is deactivated
($\neg g_{k-1}$) each round. Once every cell is covered, the exact,
unguarded equivalence is emitted; this is what allows refuting~$\ell$.

\subsection{Architecture in \cvc}

The procedure lives in an extension of the arithmetic theory
(\texttt{LiaStarExtension}), invoked at full effort with the linear solver's
candidate model. Each round it (i)~emits a non-negativity lemma and a split
on $p[\vvec]$ (if $\vvec$ itself satisfies $p$, it is a single-summand
member); (ii)~checks whether the candidate model already satisfies
$p[\vvec]$ or the last under-approximation (the \emph{model-value
shortcut}); and otherwise (iii)~runs one refinement round. Cells are
discovered from models of a persistent incremental \emph{subsolver} seeded
with $p \wedge \yvec \ge 0$ (over fresh Skolem constants $\yvec$
substituted for the lambda's bound variables) and refined with the
negations of the cells found so far.

\section{Strategies for the refinement loop}

\subsection{Accumulate vs.\ push/pop (options
\texttt{arith-liastar-push-pop})}

The baseline (\emph{accumulate}) asserts the negation of each newly
discovered cell once, monotonically; incremental mode allows repeated
\texttt{checkSat} calls. The \emph{push/pop} variant exploits the
observation that each round's refined formula $\neg(D_1 \vee \cdots \vee
D_k)$ \emph{subsumes} the previous round's: it pops the previous refined
formula and asserts the single cumulative one in a fresh frame, so the
subsolver always holds exactly two assertions and the per-assertion caches
stay bounded.

\emph{Measured:} on the hard instance the two are indistinguishable
(both time out around $10^3$ rounds and $3.2$\,GB); on the full set
accumulate is slightly ahead (463 vs.\ 460 of 480 solved at 100\,s), because
popping discards learned clauses and re-clausifies the cumulative formula
(quadratic total work). Memory was dominated by the main solver, not the
subsolver, so the push/pop advantage never materialized. \emph{Lesson:} the
suspected bottleneck (subsolver assertion accumulation) was wrong; measure
before optimizing.

\subsection{Main-solver enumeration (option
\texttt{arith-liastar-main-solver})}

This variant removes the subsolver entirely: the lambda's variables become
fresh Skolems $\yvec$ \emph{in the main solver}, constrained through a chain
of \emph{stage guards} ($h_0 \Rightarrow \mathit{base}$, and per cell $D_k$
a fresh $h_k$ with $h_k \Rightarrow h_{k-1}$ and $h_k \Rightarrow \neg
D_k$), and the next cell is read off the main solver's own candidate model.
A na\"ive completeness test (wait until the SAT solver fixes $\neg h$ at
level~0) livelocks; the working mechanism is a per-stage \emph{driver
lemma}
\[
\ell \;\Rightarrow\; \big(p[\vvec] \,\vee\, \mathit{star}_k \,\vee\, h_k\big),
\]
which is satisfiability-preserving (any model with $\vvec \in \Sstar$
outside the current approximation extends to one placing $\yvec$ in an
uncovered cell; the empty-sum member $\vvec = 0$ must be included in the
star branch) and forces an asserted-but-uncertified literal to either
certify itself or exhibit a fresh cell. An adversarial review found and we
fixed two soundness bugs: the missing empty-sum branch (wrong
\textsf{unsat} on predicates with empty $\Sset$), and enumeration lemmas
not being re-sent after user-context pops (wrong \textsf{sat} in
incremental mode), fixed by re-queueing all lemmas through the
user-context-dependent lemma cache each round.

\emph{Measured:} sound (zero discrepancies) but dominated by the subsolver
architecture (447 vs.\ 463 solved; $2.5\times$ slower on the common set):
every refinement round pays for the entire input search, and at most one
cell is harvested per full-effort check.

\subsection{Cell generalization (options
\texttt{arith-liastar-generalize}, \texttt{-generalize-semantic})}

The cell read from a model fixes the truth value of \emph{every} atom of
the predicate, making cells maximally fine: the enumeration walks the
arrangement of all atoms rather than the predicate's coarse cell structure.
\emph{Boolean generalization} computes a prime implicant of the
propositional skeleton: greedily drop literals while a three-valued
evaluation of $\mathit{base}$ under the partial assignment still yields
\emph{true} (Algorithm~\ref{alg:gen}). Soundness requires only that the
shrunken conjunction still implies $\mathit{base}$; dropping conjuncts keeps
the cell convex, the model point stays inside (so progress is preserved),
and overlapping cells are harmless because the Minkowski-sum argument needs
only $\Sset = \bigcup_j S_j$.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Boolean cell generalization (\texttt{generalizeCell})}
\label{alg:gen}
\KwIn{formula $\mathit{base}$, atoms $a_1..a_m$ with model values $b_1..b_m$}
\KwOut{keep flags $k_1..k_m$}
$\sigma \gets \{a_i \mapsto b_i\}$; \lIf{$\mathrm{eval}_3(\mathit{base},\sigma) \ne \mathit{true}$}{\Return all-keep}
\For{$i \gets 1$ \KwTo $m$}{
  $\sigma[a_i] \gets \mathit{unknown}$\;
  \leIf{$\mathrm{eval}_3(\mathit{base},\sigma) = \mathit{true}$}{$k_i \gets \mathit{false}$}{$\sigma[a_i] \gets b_i$;\; $k_i \gets \mathit{true}$}
}
\end{algorithm}

\emph{Measured:} a clear win and now the default --- cells shrink to about
half their literals, 466/480 solved (3 newly solved, none lost), and
$4.4\times$ faster on the commonly solved set.

\emph{Semantic generalization} strengthens the test to arithmetic
entailment: literal $l$ may be dropped iff $(D \setminus \{l\}) \wedge \neg
\mathit{base}$ is unsatisfiable. The implementation is core-guided: one
\texttt{checkSat} with all cell literals as \emph{assumptions} against a
persistent probe subsolver holding $\neg\mathit{base}$; the
unsat-assumptions core drops everything outside it at once, then a greedy
deletion pass (equalities first) minimizes the core. \emph{Measured:}
mechanically effective (cells halve again) but a net loss (465 solved,
$2.25\times$ slower): probe latency plus fatter cells (larger Hilbert
bases, more multipliers per cone) outweigh the extra merging. Kept
optional, default off.

\subsection{Partial sums (option \texttt{arith-liastar-partial-sums})}

Each round's reduction lemma contains sum constraints spanning \emph{all}
cones found so far, so cumulative lemma content grows quadratically in the
round count. Defining running partial-sum Skolems $P_k[i] = P_{k-1}[i] +
\mathit{contribution}_k[i]$ as unconditional lemmas (sound: they constrain
only fresh Skolems, satisfiable by zeros) collapses the guarded lemma to
the constant-size $\vvec = P_k$. \emph{Measured:} a negative result ---
memory got \emph{worse} (4.4 vs.\ 3.3\,GB on the hard instance) and 13
fewer instances solved. Two mechanisms: the per-cone definitional volume
(tens of lemmas per round) dominates with a large constant, and the
all-equality star forces dis-equality splitting when negated
($\mathit{ARITH\_SPLIT\_DEQ}$ jumped $14 \to 663$), where the fat star
offered cheap escapes (violate one multiplier bound). The
quadratic-lemma-content hypothesis for the memory wall was thereby
refuted. Kept for reference, default off.

\section{Solving satisfiable hard instances}

The hardest family (\texttt{card/fol\_0000099--0000106}) resisted every
strategy above. Diagnosis on \texttt{fol\_0000100} established three facts.
First, the instance is satisfiable but $A \wedge p[\vvec]$ is not (verified
standalone): the vector genuinely needs $\ge 2$ summands. Second, cell
discovery was \emph{not} the bottleneck once guided (below): all five
summand bit-patterns needed by a hand decomposition appear within ${\sim}30$
rounds. Third, the failure was the \emph{endgame}: concluding \textsf{sat}
requires the main solver to find integer multipliers decomposing $\vvec$
over the discovered cones --- an ILP over hundreds of Skolems --- and it
never gets to finish, because (i) the extension's check runs \emph{before}
integer branching, so fractional candidate models fail the shortcut and
trigger yet another refinement, growing the ILP under the solver's feet,
and (ii) the star's fresh equality atoms receive default SAT phases, so the
guard is \emph{propagated} false before ever being decided --- measured: the
guard was never true across 643 rounds.

Three mechanisms together solve this class (\texttt{fol\_0000100}:
timeout${} \to 0.7$\,s):

\paragraph{Guided enumeration (option \texttt{arith-liastar-guided},
default on).} Every summand of a non-negative decomposition satisfies
$\yvec \le \vvec$ componentwise. The subsolver query is therefore first
checked under the assumptions $\{ y_i \le \hat v_i \}$, where $\hat\vvec$
is the candidate model's value of $\vvec$, with an unbiased fallback when
the biased query is unsatisfiable --- so completeness (the unbiased
\textsf{unsat} signalling full coverage) is untouched. In particular,
coordinates with $\hat v_i = 0$ force $y_i = 0$, pruning the cell search
to the subspace relevant for decomposing $\vvec$.

\paragraph{Decoupled endgame (option \texttt{arith-liastar-endgame}$=N$,
default 10).} Every $N$ newly discovered cones, a \emph{fresh} subsolver
solves $F_0 \wedge \mathit{star}_k$, where $F_0$ are the arithmetic trail
facts \emph{fixed at decision level 0} (\texttt{Valuation::isFixed}) ---
using unfixed trail facts poisons the query with branch decisions (in one
measured run, the trail had pinned $n = 1$ inside a region already known
contradictory). The fresh solver runs branch-and-bound to completion,
unencumbered by stale guards, frozen phases, and lemma churn. With
guidance, it answers \textsf{sat} at ${\sim}30$ cones and yields a concrete
multiplier witness.

\paragraph{Witness feedback by decision strategy.} A guarded hint lemma $d
\Rightarrow (x_1 = c_1 \wedge \cdots)$ with $d$ phase-preferred true
\emph{never} engages: with hundreds of pinned variables, some are always
already bound differently on the trail, so $\neg d$ is propagated round
after round (measured: the hint guard was false at every single round). The
working mechanism is a \cvc \emph{decision strategy}
(\texttt{LiaStarHintStrategy}): while a hint is active, the SAT solver is
handed the guard and then each pinned equality as its next decisions,
building the witness assignment directly; one hint is active per literal
(competing witnesses pin the same Skolems to different values). The hint is
sound unconditionally --- the guard is fresh --- and once the witness
assignment is complete, the model-value shortcut accepts it.

\section{Refuting unsatisfiable hard instances: over-approximating cuts}

The four remaining timeouts (\texttt{fol\_0000098/0000107}, both
encodings) are \emph{unsatisfiable}: the lazy procedure could refute them
only by completing the enumeration, which is hopeless (the eager DNF also
times out). They need the \emph{over-approximation half} of the VMCAI
approach, which had not been implemented. Our instance of it:

\begin{proposition}[Homogeneous cuts]
If $\cvec \cdot \yvec \ge 0$ holds for every $\yvec \in \Sset$, then
$\cvec \cdot \vvec \ge 0$ holds for every $\vvec \in \Sstar$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\Sstar$ consists of finite sums of members of $\Sset$ (the empty sum being
$0$); non-negativity of a linear form is preserved under addition.
\end{proof}

Such \emph{cuts} can therefore be asserted for $\vvec$ unconditionally.
Crucially, cuts may also be \emph{conditional on the zero-forced
restriction}: if $v_i$ is the constant $0$ (which happens frequently ---
input equalities like $u_{13} = 0$ are substituted into the literal), then
every summand has $y_i = 0$, so validity needs to hold only on $\Sset \cap
\{y_i = 0\}$ --- a strictly larger space of valid cuts. The MAPA instance
\texttt{fol\_0000107} is refuted only by such a conditional cut
($4\,u_{10} - u_{14} - u_{17} - u_{20} - u_{23} - u_{26} \ge 0$, valid only
where the comparison bits are pinned to zero).

Cuts are synthesized by counterexample-guided search
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:cuts}), invoked whenever the endgame finds $F_0 \wedge
\mathit{star}_k$ unsatisfiable. Three details matter in practice:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Sparsity.} The coefficient search is constrained by an
  escalating $L_1$ budget $\sum_i |c_i| \le K$, $K \in \{4,9,16,30\}$. An
  unconstrained box admits arbitrary dense candidates that the validity
  oracle rejects one at a time; the cuts that exist in practice are sparse,
  and the budget makes the CEGIS loop converge (measured offline: 8 cuts,
  ${\sim}33$ iterations in total, refute \texttt{fol\_0000107}).
\item \emph{Sample soundness.} Module generators of discovered cones are
  genuine points of $\Sset$; those with zeros on the restricted coordinates
  lie in the restricted region and soundly constrain the search
  ($\cvec \cdot p \ge 0$). Hilbert-basis \emph{rays} must \emph{not} be
  used: a ray of an unrestricted cell has zeros on its pinned coordinates
  yet describes a direction outside the restriction, and requiring
  non-negativity on it excludes exactly the valid conditional cuts (this
  was a measured implementation bug; the counterexample loop handles
  unbounded directions soundly instead).
\item \emph{Termination of the outer loop.} Once the accumulated cuts make
  $F_0$ unsatisfiable, nothing more is needed: the cuts are ordinary
  lemmas, so the main solver derives \textsf{unsat} by itself --- no special
  reporting mechanism, hence no new soundness obligations.
\end{itemize}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Conditional cut synthesis (\texttt{synthesizeCuts}, per endgame-unsat)}
\label{alg:cuts}
\KwIn{literal $\ell$ with vector $\vvec$; fixed facts $F_0$; cuts $C$;
  samples $P$ (restricted cone generators); zero-forced coordinates $Z$}
\For{$\mathit{round} \gets 1$ \KwTo $8$}{
  \lIf{$F_0 \wedge C$ \emph{unsat}}{\Return (main solver will conclude unsat)}
  $\hat\vvec \gets$ model of $F_0 \wedge C$\;
  \ForEach{$K \in \{4, 9, 16, 30\}$}{
    \For{$\mathit{iter} \gets 1$ \KwTo $60$}{
      find $\cvec$ with $\textstyle\sum_i |c_i| \le K$,\;
      \quad $\cvec \cdot p \ge 0\ \forall p \in P$, \quad $\cvec \cdot \hat\vvec \le -1$\;
      \lIf{none}{\textbf{break} (next $K$)}
      check validity: $\mathit{base} \wedge \bigwedge_{i \in Z} y_i{=}0 \wedge \cvec\cdot\yvec \le -1$\;
      \leIf{unsat}{emit lemma $\cvec \cdot \vvec \ge 0$; add to $C$; \textbf{continue outer}}{add the model point to $P$}
    }
  }
  \lIf{no cut found}{record failure (give up after two consecutive, reset on new cones); \textbf{break}}
}
\end{algorithm}

\emph{Measured:} all four remaining instances are refuted in $0.2$--$2.0$
seconds (the conditional-cut and $L_1$ refinements were each necessary for
the MAPA variant), with no effect on the rest of the suite.

\section{Soundness summary}

Every mechanism emits only standard theory lemmas; the procedure's answers
are justified as follows. \textsf{sat} is concluded only when the candidate
model satisfies $p[\vvec]$ (single summand) or
$\mathit{star}_k$ (an under-approximation of $\Sstar$, witnessing membership);
hints and guards are fresh Booleans, so the guarded lemmas are
satisfiability-preserving, and guided/endgame components only \emph{steer}
the search. \textsf{unsat} is concluded by the main solver from
(i)~the exact equivalence once enumeration completes, or (ii)~cut lemmas,
each individually valid for $\Sstar$ (Proposition~1, restricted variant),
conflicting with the input. Generalization preserves $D \Rightarrow
\mathit{base}$ (propositionally or semantically), keeps the model point,
and tolerates overlap; the subsolver's unbiased \textsf{unsat} remains the
completeness certificate under guidance because the bias is applied only as
assumptions with a fallback.

\section{Experimental results}

All experiments: production build of \cvc with \normaliz, 480 benchmarks
(\texttt{sls-reachability}, arith/card $\times$ BAPA/MAPA encodings),
100\,s timeout, 12-way parallel harness, \texttt{OMP\_NUM\_THREADS=1},
6\,GB memory cap. No answer discrepancy was observed in any configuration,
neither among our configurations nor against the four reference solvers
recorded in the comparison data (SLS-reachability variants, an eager LIA
encoding, and two SQLSolver variants).

\section{Lessons}

Three methodological points carried the project. \emph{Measure before
optimizing}: the first two memory-motivated strategies (push/pop, partial
sums) targeted a bottleneck that profiling later refuted. \emph{Localize by
experiment}: the satisfiable-instance breakthrough came from tracing that
the needed cones were found early and that the guard was never true ---
each fix was forced by a measured failure of the previous mechanism, down
to the level of SAT-solver phase propagation. \emph{Negative results are
load-bearing}: semantic generalization and partial sums lost on this suite
but their machinery (the probe oracle, the validity-by-assumptions
pattern) became essential components of the cut synthesis that closed the
final instances.
